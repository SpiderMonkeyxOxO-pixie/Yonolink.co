## Social and team-style features

Some apps promote social or team-style play. Social features mean chat and interaction with strangers. Don't share personal details, keep conversations inside the app, and be cautious about anyone who suggests moving to another messaging platform. Treat an invitation to "play together" for money outside the app as a red flag.

## Install checks specific to rummy apps

Rummy apps ask you to enter personal details, and some ask for identity documents before withdrawals. That makes the install source more important than it is for a casual game. Before you share anything, confirm that the app's terms and privacy policy are readable, that support contact details are shown, and that the app explains what documents it may ask for and why. If an app asks for documents before telling you anything about how they are used, pause.

Keep screenshots of the terms you agreed to, with the date. If conditions change later, you will have a record of what you saw when you started.

## A short pre-play routine

Before your first session, take two minutes to read the terms, find the support contact, locate any limit or break settings and decide how much you are willing to spend. Write the number down. Having it decided in advance makes it much easier to stop when you reach it.

## Keeping your details safe

Use a phone number you control, avoid reusing passwords from other apps, and never share an OTP with anyone, including people who claim to represent the app. If a message asks you to confirm account details through a link, open the app directly instead. ## When to stop and recheck

If the installer asks for unexpected permissions, if the terms differ from what you read before downloading, or if the app pushes you to deposit quickly, stop and recheck the source before going further.
