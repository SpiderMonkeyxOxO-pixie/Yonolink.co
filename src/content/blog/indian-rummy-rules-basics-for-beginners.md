## The goal

You are dealt 13 cards. Your aim is to arrange them into valid groups and declare before your opponents.

## Key terms

- **Sequence:** three or more consecutive cards of the same suit (for example 4-5-6 of hearts).
- **Pure sequence:** a sequence with no joker.
- **Impure sequence:** a sequence that uses a joker.
- **Set:** three or four cards of the same rank in different suits.
- **Joker:** a wild card (a printed joker, or a randomly chosen "wild" rank that varies by game).

## What makes a valid declaration

1. At least **two sequences**.
2. At least **one** of them must be **pure**.
3. The remaining cards form sets or further sequences.

Without a pure sequence, a declaration is invalid.

## How a turn goes

1. Draw a card from the closed deck or the open pile.
2. Decide what to keep.
3. Discard one card.
4. Repeat until someone declares.

## A simple example hand

Suppose your hand is arranged as: 4♥ 5♥ 6♥ (a pure sequence), 9♠ 10♠ J♠ (a second pure sequence), 7♦ 7♣ 7♠ (a set), and 2♣ K♦ Joker with a few cards left to place. You would keep working to fit the remaining cards into a group before declaring. The two sequences are already valid, and one is pure, so the key requirement is met. Only the leftover cards stand between you and a valid declaration.

## Scoring in simple terms

Players who didn't declare count points for unmatched cards. Face cards and aces usually count 10 each, and numbered cards count their value. Many formats cap the penalty. Details vary by format and app, so read the rules shown in the app.

## Common formats

- **Points rummy:** one deal, fixed point value.
- **Pool rummy:** accumulate points toward a limit.
- **Deals rummy:** a fixed number of deals.

Not every app offers every format.

## Skill and luck

Memory, discarding strategy and probability help. But the cards you are dealt are random, and no strategy guarantees an outcome. That balance is why legal treatment of rummy has varied, and why rules for real-money versions in India have been changing. Check current law before playing for money, and note that players must be 18+.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Chasing the first sequence only.** Keep your options open until you know which cards you can combine.
- **Holding high cards too long.** If a hand goes badly, face cards and aces count heavily against you.
- **Forgetting the pure sequence.** Many first-time players build impressive sets and then cannot declare.
- **Discarding without watching.** The cards your opponents pick up and drop tell you what they are building.
- **Playing too fast.** Most apps have a turn timer, but a few extra seconds spent thinking usually beat a rushed discard.

Practising in a free mode, where one exists, is the cheapest way to learn all of this.
