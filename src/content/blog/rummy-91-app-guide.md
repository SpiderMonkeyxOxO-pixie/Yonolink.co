## The 91% RTP claim, explained

RTP (Return to Player) describes, in theory, how much of all money wagered across a platform comes back to players as winnings over time. A 91% RTP means that for every ₹100 wagered in aggregate, ₹91 is returned as winnings on average — the platform's take, in theory, is the remaining 9%. This is a statistical average across a large volume of play, not a promise about any individual session: you can lose your entire deposit on a 91%-RTP table in one bad run, and the figure says nothing about variance, luck, or short-term outcomes.

The harder question is verification. A genuinely independent RTP audit would normally mean a named third-party firm reviewing the game's random number generation and payout logs against a public methodology, with findings a player could check. Rummy 91's own description uses the word "independently audited" and "monthly audit reports," but doesn't name the auditor, the methodology, or where the reports are hosted in a way we could confirm — and we were unable to reach a working copy of the stated reports ourselves. That doesn't mean the claim is false. It means it's currently a claim, not a confirmed fact, and the two are different things even when a number sounds precise and authoritative.

The 2% rake cap and blockchain-timestamped shuffling claims sit in the same category: specific-sounding, plausible, and entirely unverified by us. A rake cap tells you the house's maximum table commission, not your actual win rate — a low rake and a low RTP can coexist on the same platform if the game's internal odds are built that way, so don't treat "2% rake" as evidence toward the separate 91% RTP figure. They're different numbers measuring different things.
